How Lamborghini
was built

The story of a tractor maker who decided to build the best sports car in the world.

1916

Ferruccio Lamborghini is born into a farming family near Bologna, Italy. He grows up loving machines.

1948

After working as a mechanic in the Italian air force during World War II, he starts building tractors from leftover military vehicles. The business becomes a big success.

1963

He founds Automobili Lamborghini in Sant’Agata Bolognese. A popular story says he decided to build his own cars after a disagreement with Enzo Ferrari about a Ferrari he owned, though historians debate the details.

1964

The 350 GT goes on sale, powered by a V12 designed by engineer Giotto Bizzarrini.

1966

The Miura shocks the world. A young team of engineers, Gian Paolo Dallara, Paolo Stanzani and Bob Wallace, helped develop it, and it put the engine behind the driver like a race car.

1972–1974

Money problems and a tough economy push Ferruccio to sell his shares. He retires to a wine estate in Umbria and later passes away in 1993.

1974

The wedge-shaped Countach arrives, with scissor doors that become a Lamborghini trademark.

1998

After several owners, Audi, part of the Volkswagen Group, takes over. Sales and quality grow.

2018–2023

The Urus SUV becomes a hit, and in 2023 Lamborghini delivers more than 10,000 cars in a single year for the first time.

Fun facts

Things that make Lamborghini different from every other car maker.

Bulls everywhere

Ferruccio was born under the zodiac sign Taurus and loved bullfighting. The logo is a golden bull, and most models are named after fighting bulls or bull breeders.

Still a tractor brand

The tractor company still exists today, but it has been owned by someone else for a long time and has nothing to do with the car maker.

Scissor doors

Lamborghini’s V12 flagship cars open their doors upward since the Countach, which is a big part of why they look so dramatic.

A V12 that lasted decades

The V12 engine family that started in 1963 was developed over nearly five decades before an all-new V12 arrived with the Aventador.

Engine behind you

Since the Miura, most Lamborghini supercars place the engine behind the driver, which helps balance and handling.

Names with meaning

Murciélago means “bat”, Espada means “sword”, and Urus is named after the aurochs, the wild ancestor of modern cattle.

Same home since 1963

Lamborghini still builds its cars at its factory in Sant’Agata Bolognese, near Bologna in Italy.

Going electric

The Revuelto is the first plug-in hybrid V12 Lamborghini, and the Temerario also uses a hybrid system.
